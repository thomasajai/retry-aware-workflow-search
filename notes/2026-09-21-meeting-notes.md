## Discussion and working direction

1. **Separate the objectives and costs.** For a single-objective experiment, simple regret was described as oracle accuracy minus the accuracy of the recommended configuration; plot it against cumulative *search/profiling* expense. *Deployment cost* is the per-use cost of the chosen configuration, a separate quantity. A multi-objective version would seek an accuracy/cost Pareto frontier. The whiteboard and first notebook page show both the regret-versus-search-cost sketch and the cost/accuracy frontier.
2. **Measure cost rather than assuming it is known.** API prices are published per input and output token, but output length is unknown before a call and benchmark tasks can have very different response lengths. Warm-up batches or initial measurements can estimate both latent cost and accuracy. Repeated model switching may lose cache benefits, particularly in retry loops. The group also discussed whether latency should mean provider/model time, time to first token, or end-to-end round-trip time; this needs an explicit choice in the experiment.
3. **Keep the first research scope tractable.** The discussion continued the earlier recommendation to start with a single objective while recording cost and latency for later multi-objective analysis. Harness selection or design was raised as an alternative research direction. At the end of the discussion, the team still planned to compare the two directions and decide after more reading; the project brief supplied for this workspace now specifies retry-aware search. The advisor cautioned that building a new algorithm, collecting a new retry dataset, and implementing a system all at once would be too much for the first milestone.
4. **Choose a representative benchmark and a small workflow.** A result found on one benchmark does not automatically generalize; the evaluation questions should reflect the intended application. The group discussed starting with **one benchmark**, a limited set of inexpensive candidate models, and possibly a solver/verifier workflow with a small retry limit. Existing AgentOpt workflows and LangChain-based implementation were mentioned as practical starting points. This was a scoping suggestion and apparent working agreement, not a finalized experiment specification.
5. **Distinguish the verifier's feedback from ground truth.** A verifier can mistakenly reject a correct solver answer or accept an incorrect one. Its judgment can trigger retries, but final benchmark accuracy should be scored against independent ground truth. A retry cap is needed so failures remain possible and cost remains bounded. The trigger, cap, and fallback model policy are still to be chosen.
6. **Collect attempt-level data.** The earlier AgentOpt/BFCL experiment reportedly kept aggregate cost but not the realized number of retries. The group discussed storing enough information to replay or analyze each run: responses, correctness, retry count, model choice, token use, cost, and timing. The advisor suggested that extending the system to collect this data and reporting substantive findings could itself be a useful course result before a novel search algorithm is ready. Storage volume and engineering effort are tradeoffs.
7. **Related work and baselines.** The three main papers named by the team are [AgentOpt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06296), [VineLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23914), and [SCOPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00774). The discussion also named [SySRs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07726) (evaluate remaining models on shared questions and eliminate weak ones), [BanditEval / *On Speeding Up Language Model Evaluation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06172) (including UCB-E and low-rank factorization), and [PromptEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17202). The group discussed random search and exhaustive evaluation on a small space as references; on a large space, exhaustive ground truth may be unaffordable, so accuracy against other methods can be reported without oracle regret. No final 1–2 baseline choice was recorded.
8. **Access and budget are unresolved.** The group asked how much API budget and Amazon Bedrock access the course provides. Existing response matrices could reduce collection cost, while a retry-aware workflow may need fresh measurements. Cheaper models were proposed for building and debugging the harness before expensive profiling. The meeting did not establish a budget or grant access.
